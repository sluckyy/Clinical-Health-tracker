Why the arithmetic works

The “we can’t fill ED” claim was priced against ten roster lines a day, not five

The actual roster runs up to 10 on-call lines every day: four ED lines (Crystal Brook, Jamestown, Laura, Orroroo — Laura’s line quietly stopped being rostered in August) plus six obstetric roles: GPA, obs on-call and obs support at both sites. The consolidated model needs 5: two ED lines (JT covering Orroroo remotely, CB covering Laura) plus one maternity team at the active birthing site.

Fifteen ED-credentialed doctors appear on the actual roster. With only three of them tied up on maternity call on any given day, the two ED lines share comfortably — most ED doctors land on 4–14 nights a month in the generated rosters. The solver confirms it day by day, not on averages: zero unfillable ED nights in any scenario, including a month with a GPO, a GPA and an obs-support doctor away simultaneously.

The hidden cost of the status quo

The old roster only “works” by multi-hatting and quiet holes

In July alone, doctors covered two or three lines at once on 75 person-days — Simon Jackson holding Jamestown ED, Jamestown obs and Orroroo ED on the same day; Rowena Conway holding both sites’ GPA lines simultaneously for weeks. A multi-hatted day is exactly the situation behind the Orroroo cord-prolapse near miss. And the “filled” roster already leaks: 14 unfilled cells and 2 GPA DIVERT-to-Pirie days in July, locums patching ED and even the obs on-call line in August. The new model eliminates multi-hatting as a hard rule and flags any true gap in red rather than hiding it.

How the generator works

Rules in, roster out — regenerate in under a minute

Hard rules (never broken)

  • One role per doctor per day — ED and maternity can never sit on the same head; the actual roster’s multi-hatting is switched off.
  • Skills match the role — GPO/GPA/obs-support lines only go to doctors who actually work those lines today.
  • Leave is absolute — a doctor on leave is simply not in the pool.
  • Fatigue cap — no more than 4 consecutive on-call days for anyone (the actual roster contains a 31-day stretch).
  • Every role covered every day — or the day is flagged in red, never silently dropped.

Preferences (optimised, tweakable)

  • Reduced-load doctors steered to ~55% of a fair share — this is what gets Holly and Simon to 7 days.
  • Fair-share targets scaled by FTE, with penalties for drifting above.
  • Maternity continuity — week-style blocks for GPO/GPA/obs support, fewer handovers.
  • Home-site ED preference — the JT line leans on JT doctors (Orroroo link), CB on CB doctors (Laura link).
  • Clare backup last — the GPO/GPA/obs-support fallback pool is used only when the local roster genuinely can’t cover, and every use is counted and shown.

The workforce (names, skills, FTEs, load flags) lives in rostering/workforce_config.json; months, leave and the active birthing site live in rostering/scenarios.json; python3 roster_solver.py rebuilds every generated roster on this page. What this page is — and isn’t

The baseline is now the real roster. July and August 2026 actuals are parsed straight from the roster spreadsheet, including its typos, its “Rowena Conway (SJ)/(HD)” backup annotations (counted as Rowena, with the bracketed doctor treated as backstop), Michelle and Ben as locums, and the DIVERT days. Weekend shading uses the roster’s own weekday row. Future-month leave is assumed for the demo — swap in real requests before circulating individual numbers.
FTE estimates are inferred, not payroll. Doctors who appear rarely on the actual roster (Rick, Gareth, Nikki, Cynthia, Josh, Nitya) are modelled part-time so the fairness targets are realistic. If someone’s availability is materially different, it’s one number in the config.
Laura and Orroroo are folded into the CB and JT ED lines, matching the stated practice (CB covers Laura remotely, JT covers Orroroo) and the fact that Laura’s separate line was already unrostered in August. Reliable Webex/DTN from doctors’ homes makes this fold safer and benefits either model. If separate lines must stay, the pool is 15 ED doctors for 4 lines — the generator can be re-run that way.
Consolidation helps, but doesn’t solve, the birthing-experience finding. Fewer than 50 births a year split across two teams means each roster line attends very few. One team concentrates that exposure, but ~50/year is still low. Structured Port Pirie rotations are the credible answer for accreditation — and the new roster’s slack (Holly and Simon at 7 days, obs-support doctors at 4–13) is what makes them schedulable at all.
