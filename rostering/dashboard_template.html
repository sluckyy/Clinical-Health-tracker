Why the arithmetic works

The “we can’t fill ED” claim was priced against six obstetric roles, not three

The old two-site model needs 8 on-call roles every day: two ED lines (JT covering Orroroo, CB covering Laura) plus a full GPO + GPA + obs-support team at both sites. The consolidated model needs 5: the same two ED lines plus one maternity team at the active birthing site.

With ten ED-credentialed doctors, three on maternity call on any given day, and typical leave, roughly six to seven doctors remain available for the two ED lines — about 11–16 ED nights per doctor per month, inside normal rural on-call territory. The roster generator below confirms it day by day rather than on averages: it found zero unfillable ED nights in any scenario, including a month with two of three GPOs and a GPA away simultaneously.

The hidden cost of the status quo

The old roster only “works” by double-hatting and backfill

On paper the old roster covers ED too — but only because doctors routinely hold an ED line and an obstetric role at the same time, and because 23–32 person-days a month of maternity cover must come from outside the local workforce. A double-hatted day is exactly the situation behind the Orroroo cord-prolapse near miss: the on-call GPO physically at an outlying site while carrying the obstetric pager. The new model eliminates it as a hard rule, not a hope.

How the generator works

Rules in, roster out — regenerate in under a minute

Hard rules (never broken)

  • One role per doctor per day — ED and maternity can never sit on the same head (the old model’s double-hatting is switched off).
  • Skills match the role — only credentialed GPOs/GPAs/obs-support doctors are rostered to those lines.
  • Leave is absolute — a doctor on leave is simply not in the pool.
  • Fatigue cap — no more than 4 consecutive on-call days for anyone.
  • Every role covered every day — or the day is flagged in red, never silently dropped.

Preferences (optimised, tweakable)

  • Reduced-load doctors are steered to ~55% of a fair share — this is what gets Holly D and Simon J to 8 days.
  • Fair-share targets scaled by FTE, with penalties for anyone drifting above.
  • Maternity continuity — week-style blocks for GPO/GPA/obs support, fewer handovers.
  • Home-site ED preference — the JT line leans on JT doctors (Orroroo link), CB on CB doctors (Laura link).
  • Clare backup last — the GPO/GPA/obs-support fallback pool is used only when the local roster genuinely can’t, and every use is counted and shown.

Everything above lives in two plain files — rostering/workforce_config.json (doctors, skills, FTE, load flags) and rostering/scenarios.json (month, leave requests, active birthing site) — and python3 roster_solver.py rebuilds every roster on this page. Add Lauren in month 6 or Krishna in month 12 by moving one entry; add a leave request with one line. Occasional gaps get tweaked by hand, exactly as intended.

What this demo is — and isn’t

The workforce here is a realistic stand-in, not the real July/August roster. The attached spreadsheets didn’t reach this analysis, so ten doctors with a plausible JT/CB skill mix (3 GPOs, 2 GPAs, 3 obs-support-capable, all ED-credentialed) were modelled. The structure of the argument — 5 roles/day vs 8, no double-hatting, solver-verified coverage — does not depend on the stand-in; the exact per-doctor numbers do. Drop the real workforce into the config and regenerate before circulating numbers beyond the meeting.
Consolidation helps, but doesn’t solve, the birthing-experience finding. Fewer than 50 births a year split across two teams means each roster line attends very few. One team concentrates that exposure — the same births across half as many rostered clinicians — but ~50/year is still low. Structured rotations through Port Pirie (and the Clare link working both ways) are the credible answer to accreditation, and the alternating-site model makes them easier to roster, not harder.
Remote cover for Laura and Orroroo is a separate, worthwhile fix. Reliable Webex/DTN from doctors’ homes reduces the driving that made the old double-hatted model dangerous, and it benefits either roster model.
